{# SYSTEM SHEET — the mobile home for every destination the bottom nav does not carry. WHAT IT REPLACES ---------------- The header used to carry a hamburger that expanded `#navbarNav` into a full-screen stack of dropdown menus, sitting next to a five-tab bottom bar. Two competing navigation systems on one screen is the anti-pattern: the same question ("where can I go?") had two different answers in two different shapes, and the collapsed menu was the slow path for everything the bar did not cover. The bottom bar keeps the five destinations people reach for constantly. This sheet is for the rest, and it is the ONLY place they live on a phone, so there is one answer rather than two. WHY AN OFF-CANVAS BOTTOM SHEET, NOT A COLLAPSE ---------------------------------------------- * A bottom sheet comes from the same edge as the bar that opened it, so it reads as an extension of the bar. A collapse from the top would make the user's eye cross the whole screen and back. * It keeps Bootstrap's focus trap, Escape handling and backdrop for free — the mobile nav needs all three, and the previous collapse gave none of them (a full-screen menu with no focus management). * It is NOT a `navbar-collapse`, so `--navbar-height` does not change when it is open. That matters: the flicker guard in main.js measures the navbar and subtracts the open collapse, and a second collapse-shaped overlay would have made that accounting ambiguous. PARITY WITH THE DESKTOP NAVBAR ------------------------------ The destinations here mirror the navbar's dropdowns. The duplication is deliberate — the two need different markup (flat list vs. nested menus) — so tests/test_mobile_chrome_redesign.py asserts that every `url_for` endpoint in the desktop nav also appears here. Dropping a destination from either place fails the suite instead of silently stranding it. #}
